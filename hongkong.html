The week at a glance

Busy days, then slow ones

Every full day is followed by an easier one, so you never stack two big outings back to back without a breather. Disneyland and the beach both land on weekdays, when crowds are thinner.

尖沙咀

Day 1 · Sunday

Land, rest, harbour lights

Tsim Sha Tsui, Kowloon

  1. Morning

    Arrive and check in 機場快綫

    Take the Airport Express into town, then a short taxi to your hotel. Tsim Sha Tsui is the most convenient base for this week. If the room isn't ready, leave the bags and get a coffee; the afternoon is for napping.

    Getting thereKowloon station on the Airport Express, then taxi

  2. 4:30 PM

    Avenue of Stars 星光大道

    The waterfront promenade facing the Hong Kong Island skyline. A slow walk with no agenda, and a good way to get your bearings.

    Getting thereEast Tsim Sha Tsui Exit J, a few minutes' walk

  3. 6:00 PM

    Dinner at Sangeetha

    Fully vegetarian South Indian. Order the dosa platter or a masala dosa. Vegan and Jain versions are available on request.

    WhereWing On Plaza, 62 Mody RoadEast Tsim Sha Tsui Exit P1 · dinner 6–10 PM

  4. 8:00 PM

    A Symphony of Lights 幻彩詠香江

    A ten-minute light show across the harbour's towers, best watched from the Avenue of Stars.

Check the light show before you go. The government plans to retire A Symphony of Lights in the last quarter of 2026 and replace it with festival light shows; no final date has been announced. It is also cancelled when typhoon signal No. 3 or higher, or a red or black rainstorm warning, is in force. If it has ended, the harbour walk is still the best way to spend the evening.
太平山

Day 2 · Monday

The Peak and old Central

Hong Kong Island

  1. 9:00 AM

    Peak Tram to Victoria Peak 山頂纜車

    A steep 1.4 km climb in about eight minutes. Go early, before the queue and the midday haze. The Sky Terrace 428 viewing deck at the top opens at 8:30 AM.

    Getting thereCentral Exit J2, then about 10 minutes uphill to the Garden Road terminus. Buy tickets at the counter; Octopus can't be tapped for entry.

  2. 12:00 PM

    Lunch at LockCha Tea House 樂茶軒

    All-vegetarian dim sum and more than a hundred teas, inside the restored Tai Kwun police compound. The pan-fried taro cake is the one to get.

    WhereTai Kwun, 10 Hollywood Road · weekdays from 11 AM · book on their site

  3. 1:30 PM

    Hollywood Road and Man Mo Temple 文武廟

    Look around Tai Kwun's courtyards, then walk west past the antique and gallery shops to the incense-filled temple, open 8 AM–6 PM.

    Getting thereWalk. The Mid-Levels escalator runs uphill from 10 AM if you want a lift.

  4. 3:30 PM

    PMQ 元創方

    The former police married quarters, now small studios and shops by local designers. A good first look at decor pieces before the shopping days.

    Where35 Aberdeen Street, a few minutes' walk from Man Mo Temple

  5. 5:30 PM

    Star Ferry back to Kowloon 天星小輪

    A short crossing with the best view in the city, for about HK$5 on the upper deck. Pay with Octopus or a card at the machines.

    WhereCentral Pier 7 to Tsim Sha Tsui · boats every 6–12 minutes

  6. 7:00 PM

    Dinner at Kailash Parbat

    Vegetarian Indian from a Mumbai institution founded in 1952. Pav bhaji, chole bhature and pani puri; vegan and Jain menus too.

    Where3/F Multifield Plaza, 3–7A Prat AvenueTsim Sha Tsui

淺水灣

Day 3 · Tuesday

Beach morning, spa afternoon

Southside, Hong Kong Island

  1. 10:00 AM

    Repulse Bay Beach 淺水灣

    A wide crescent of sand with calm water and cafés at your back. On a weekday morning you'll have plenty of room. Bring towels and sunscreen and plan to do very little.

    Getting thereBus 6, 6X or 260 from Exchange SquareCentral · or bus 973 from Tsim Sha Tsui · about 20 minutes by taxi from Central

  2. 12:30 PM

    Lunch by the beach

    Spices, in The Repulse Bay arcade, has a separate vegan and vegetarian menu and outdoor tables. Classified, at The Pulse right on the sand, is a casual café with vegetarian options.

    Where109 Repulse Bay Road · The Pulse, 28 Beach Road

  3. 2:30 PM

    Spa day

    A couples massage and a long, slow afternoon. We'll pick the place later; book a couples room a few days ahead so the time slot is yours.

  4. Evening

    Quiet night

    An early dinner near the hotel and a proper night's sleep before Disneyland.

Lifeguards at Repulse Bay are on duty about 9 AM–6 PM from April to October, 8 AM–5 PM in November and March, and not at all from December to February. If your trip falls in winter, neighbouring Deep Water Bay is staffed year-round.
迪士尼

Day 4 · Wednesday

Hong Kong Disneyland

Lantau Island

  1. Opening

    Be at the gates when the park opens 香港迪士尼樂園

    Midweek is generally calmer than the weekend, and one wait-time analysis found Wednesday the quietest day. Head straight to World of Frozen while the queues are short, then work back toward Main Street.

    Getting thereMTR to Sunny Bay then change to the Disneyland Resort line · about 30 minutes from Hong Kong or Kowloon station

  2. 12:30 PM

    Lunch at Plaza Inn

    A Chinese table-service restaurant on Main Street with vegetarian dishes marked on the menu. You can reserve online. For something quick, Starliner Diner in Tomorrowland has a veggie burger.

  3. Afternoon

    Rides, then a break

    Mid-afternoon is the busiest stretch. Use it for a sit-down show or a cold drink in the shade so you have energy left for the evening.

  4. Closing

    Momentous nighttime spectacular

    About 20 minutes of fireworks and projections over the castle. Find a spot on Main Street early. Showtimes change by season, so check the park calendar for your date.

Book the date, not just the ticket. Every guest needs a park reservation for a specific day, up to 90 days ahead, and each ticket type has a daily quota. If typhoon signal No. 8 goes up, the park closes; Friday's Lantau day can swap in.
深水埗

Day 5 · Thursday

Tech, fabric and markets

Sham Shui Po, Jordan and Mong Kok, Kowloon

  1. 10:00 AM

    Apliu Street 鴨寮街

    An open-air electronics flea market: cables, chargers, cameras, headphones and odd gadgets laid out on tables.

    Getting thereSham Shui Po Exit C2

  2. 11:00 AM

    Golden Computer Arcade 黃金電腦商場

    Floors of computer parts, accessories and peripherals. Compare prices at a few stalls before you buy.

    WhereFuk Wa Street, a few minutes' walk · about 10 AM–8 PM

  3. 12:00 PM

    The fabric streets 基隆街

    Ki Lung, Nam Cheong and Yu Chau streets: bolts of fabric, trims, ribbons, beads and cushion covers. Good for home projects and small decor finds.

  4. 1:30 PM

    Lunch at Three Virtues 三德素食館

    Vegetarian Cantonese food and dim sum; order the pan-fried turnip cake. Listings disagree on hours, so phone ahead.

    WhereJD Mall, 233–239 Nathan RoadJordan · tel. 3622 1888

  5. 3:00 PM

    Mong Kok: phones, sneakers, Ladies' Market 旺角

    Phones and accessories at Sin Tat Plaza on Argyle Street, then every trainer imaginable along Fa Yuen Street, then clothes and souvenirs on Tung Choi Street. Bargaining is normal at the market stalls.

    Getting thereMong Kok Exit E2 for the Ladies' Market

  6. Evening

    Harbour City, if you still have energy 海港城

    Brand-name clothes in one of the city's biggest malls, a short walk from most Tsim Sha Tsui hotels. Dinner at whichever of Sunday's or Monday's spots you liked best.

大嶼山

Day 6 · Friday

Cable car, Big Buddha, Tai O

Lantau Island

  1. 10:00 AM

    Ngong Ping 360 cable car 昂坪360

    A 25-minute ride over the mountains and sea. Crystal cabins have glass floors; Crystal+ adds glass sides.

    Getting thereTung Chung Exit B, a 5-minute walk · runs about 10 AM–6 PM

  2. 11:00 AM

    Tian Tan Buddha 天壇大佛

    268 steps up to the giant bronze Buddha, with views across Lantau's peaks and the sea.

  3. 12:30 PM

    Vegetarian lunch at Po Lin Monastery 寶蓮禪寺

    A fixed vegetarian set (soup, a fried dish, vegetables, rice and tea) that changes with the season. Buy a meal ticket at the kitchen reception or the Buddha's entrance; the standard set is around HK$148 a person. Tofu pudding from the kiosk next door makes a good dessert.

    HoursKitchen 11:30 AM–4:30 PM

  4. 2:30 PM

    Tai O fishing village 大澳

    Stilt houses over the water, dried-seafood stalls and a much slower pace than the city.

    Getting thereBus 21 from Ngong Ping, about 15 minutes · bus back to Tung Chung

  5. Evening

    Quiet night in

    Room service or something simple nearby. Tomorrow is the last full day.

Check np360.com.hk a week before. The cable car closes for a few days of scheduled maintenance several times a year. If it's shut, take bus 23 from Tung Chung up to Ngong Ping instead.

Day 7 · Saturday

Boutiques, big malls, farewell dinner

Wan Chai and Causeway Bay

  1. 10:30 AM

    Starstreet Precinct 星街小區

    Star, Moon and Sun streets: small cafés, boutiques and home shops on quiet sloping lanes. Time Out named it one of the world's coolest neighbourhoods in September 2026.

    Getting thereAdmiralty through the Three Pacific Place link

  2. 12:30 PM

    Lunch at Isoya

    Japanese vegetarian: sushi, tempura and tofu dishes. Set lunches start around HK$98.

    Where1/F, 7 Mallory StreetWan Chai · Mon–Sat, 12–3 PM

  3. 2:00 PM

    Ding-ding tram to Causeway Bay 電車

    Sit on the upper deck as it rattles east along Hennessy Road. Board at the back and tap Octopus as you get off at the front.

  4. 2:30 PM

    Causeway Bay malls 銅鑼灣

    Hysan Place, Lee Garden and Times Square, all within a few minutes of each other: clothes, beauty and home goods. Good for anything still on the list.

    Getting thereCauseway Bay

  5. 7:00 PM

    Farewell dinner at Veggie Kingdom 緻素坊

    Vegetarian Cantonese cooking built on vegetables, tofu and mushrooms rather than mock meat. Book ahead; it's a Saturday.

    Where7/F Kyoto Plaza, 491–499 Lockhart RoadCauseway Bay · tel. 2366 9223

Day 8 · Sunday

Slow breakfast, then home

A last walk along the waterfront, then the Airport Express: 24 minutes from Hong Kong Station. Hong Kong and Kowloon stations offer in-town check-in for some airlines, including Cathay Pacific, Hong Kong Airlines, Qantas and Singapore Airlines, from 24 hours to 90 minutes before departure. Drop your bags there in the morning and travel to the airport light.

Eating vegetarian

The vegetarian table

Hong Kong is easier for vegetarians than it looks, as long as you know where meat and fish hide. At ordinary Cantonese restaurants, stir-fried greens usually come with oyster sauce, and chicken powder and dried shrimp turn up as background seasoning. Dedicated vegetarian restaurants, marked 齋 or 素, remove the guesswork.

楊枝甘露

Mango pomelo sago

Chilled mango, pomelo and sago in coconut milk. The dessert to order everywhere.

Vegetarian

Buddha's delight

Braised vegetables, mushrooms, tofu skin and glass noodles. Order it at vegetarian restaurants.

Bakery classics, but traditional egg-tart pastry is made with lard, and so is the crackly topping on many pineapple buns.

Ask first

Four phrases

  • 我食齋ngo5 sik6 zaai1
    I'm vegetarian.
  • 我唔食肉ngo5 m4 sik6 juk6
    I don't eat meat.
  • 走蠔油zau2 hou4 jau4
    No oyster sauce.
  • 唔該m4 goi1
    Please, or thank you (for a service).

Show this to your server

"Excuse me, we're vegetarian. Please don't add any meat, seafood, oyster sauce or chicken powder."

Two shopping days

What to buy where

Hong Kong has no sales tax, so the price on the tag is the price you pay. Thursday covers Kowloon's markets and specialist streets; Saturday covers the Island's boutiques and malls.

Logistics

Getting around

The MTR gets you almost everywhere on this plan. Ferries, trams and buses fill the gaps, and taxis are cheap by US standards.

Pay for everything

Octopus card

Download the Octopus App for Tourists and add a card to Apple Pay with a US Visa or Mastercard (iPhone X or later). Or buy a physical Sold Tourist Octopus card (HK$39, then add value). It works on the MTR, buses, trams, the Star Ferry and at 7-Eleven. MTR gates also take contactless Visa, Mastercard and Apple Pay directly. The Peak Tram is the exception: buy a ticket at the counter.

On the water and the rails

Star Ferry and trams

The Star Ferry runs between Central Pier 7 and Tsim Sha Tsui every 6–12 minutes, from early morning to about 11:30 PM. The double-decker trams run along the north shore of the Island; board at the back and pay as you leave. Both take Octopus.

Before you go

Practical notes

Entry

US passport holders can visit Hong Kong without a visa for up to 90 days.

Book ahead

Disneyland (ticket plus a dated park reservation), the spa, Saturday dinner at Veggie Kingdom, and LockCha if you want a set time.

Typhoons

Typhoon season runs May to November. Under signal No. 8, Disneyland closes, buses and ferries stop, and the MTR keeps running with a reduced service on open-air stretches. The Disney and Lantau days are the ones to swap if a storm comes through.

Holidays and crowds

Golden Week (about 1–7 October) brings big mainland crowds. Public holidays later in 2026: Monday 19 October, and 25–26 December.

Money and tipping

Many restaurants add a 10% service charge, and no further tip is expected. Small cafés and stalls don't expect tips; rounding up a taxi fare is a nice gesture.

Power

UK-style three-pin plugs (Type G), 220V. Phone and laptop chargers are usually fine with an adapter; check anything that says 110V only.

The spa

Tuesday afternoon is set aside for it. Pick the place once the hotel is booked, so it's an easy trip from the beach or back to the room.

Confirm hours

Everything here was checked in October 2026. Opening times, prices and attraction schedules change, so confirm the big ones the week you travel.
