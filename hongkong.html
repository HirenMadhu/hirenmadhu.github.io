The week at a glance

Busy days, then slow ones

This is the busiest week of Hong Kong's year, with two public holidays (26 December and 1 January) and New Year's Eve in the middle. The plan puts the beach, Disneyland and Lantau on the working days in between, keeps New Year's Eve afternoon free for a nap, and leaves New Year's Day slow.

尖沙咀

Day 1 · Saturday 26 December

Land, rest, Christmas lights

Tsim Sha Tsui, Kowloon · public holiday

  1. Morning

    Arrive and check in 機場快綫

    Take the Airport Express into town, then a short taxi to your hotel. Tsim Sha Tsui is the most convenient base for this week, and for New Year's Eve it puts you a short walk from the waterfront. If the room isn't ready, leave the bags and get a coffee; the afternoon is for napping.

    Getting thereKowloon station on the Airport Express, then taxi

  2. 4:30 PM

    Avenue of Stars 星光大道

    The waterfront promenade facing the Hong Kong Island skyline. A slow walk with no agenda, and a chance to scout where you'll stand on New Year's Eve.

    Getting thereEast Tsim Sha Tsui Exit J, a few minutes' walk

  3. 6:00 PM

    Dinner at Sangeetha

    Fully vegetarian South Indian. Order the dosa platter or a masala dosa. Vegan and Jain versions are available on request. Book, since it's a holiday.

    WhereWing On Plaza, 62 Mody RoadEast Tsim Sha Tsui Exit P1 · dinner 6–10 PM

  4. 7:30 PM

    East Tsim Sha Tsui Christmas lights

    In past years the towers around Mody Road, Wing On Plaza included, have been lit for Christmas from late November to early January, 6–11 PM, with a light corridor along Mody Road. You'll be standing in it after dinner.

  5. 8:00 PM

    Harbour light show 幻彩詠香江

    The nightly Symphony of Lights is due to be retired before the end of 2026, and a new festival light show may run over the Christmas season instead. Check the Tourism Board's site that week. Either way, the skyline from the promenade is the view.

太平山

Day 2 · Sunday 27 December

The Peak, Central and Christmas Town

Hong Kong Island

  1. 9:00 AM

    Peak Tram to Victoria Peak 山頂纜車

    A steep 1.4 km climb in about eight minutes. On a Sunday in holiday week the queue builds fast, so be there by 9. The Sky Terrace 428 viewing deck opens at 8:30 AM.

    Getting thereCentral Exit J2, then about 10 minutes uphill to the Garden Road terminus. Buy tickets at the counter; Octopus can't be tapped for entry.

  2. 12:00 PM

    Lunch at LockCha Tea House 樂茶軒

    All-vegetarian dim sum and more than a hundred teas, inside the restored Tai Kwun police compound. The pan-fried taro cake is the one to get.

    WhereTai Kwun, 10 Hollywood Road · weekends from 8 AM · book on their site

  3. 1:30 PM

    Hollywood Road and Man Mo Temple 文武廟

    Look around Tai Kwun's courtyards, then walk west past the antique and gallery shops to the incense-filled temple, open 8 AM–6 PM.

    Getting thereWalk. The Mid-Levels escalator runs uphill from 10 AM if you want a lift.

  4. 3:30 PM

    PMQ 元創方

    The former police married quarters, now small studios and shops by local designers. A good first look at decor pieces.

    Where35 Aberdeen Street, a few minutes' walk from Man Mo Temple

  5. 5:30 PM

    WinterFest Christmas Town 皇后像廣場

    The Tourism Board's 20-metre Christmas tree and Christmas Town in Statue Square Gardens. Last year it stayed open until 11:30 PM in the final week of December, with a light show every 30 minutes after dark.

    Getting thereCentral a few minutes' walk

  6. 7:00 PM

    Star Ferry back to Kowloon 天星小輪

    A short crossing with the lit-up skyline behind you, for a few Hong Kong dollars. Pay with Octopus or a card at the machines.

    WhereCentral Pier 7 to Tsim Sha Tsui · boats every 6–12 minutes

  7. 7:45 PM

    Dinner at Kailash Parbat

    Vegetarian Indian from a Mumbai institution founded in 1952. Pav bhaji, chole bhature and pani puri; vegan and Jain menus too.

    Where3/F Multifield Plaza, 3–7A Prat AvenueTsim Sha Tsui

淺水灣

Day 3 · Monday 28 December

Beach morning, spa afternoon

Southside, Hong Kong Island

  1. 10:30 AM

    Repulse Bay Beach 淺水灣

    Late December is mild by Hong Kong standards: highs around 20°C (68°F), and the sea is about the same. Think a long walk on the sand and a coffee facing the water rather than a swim. If you do want a dip, Deep Water Bay along the Seaview Promenade has lifeguards all winter.

    Getting thereBus 6, 6X or 260 from Exchange SquareCentral · or bus 973 from Tsim Sha Tsui · about 20 minutes by taxi from Central

  2. 12:30 PM

    Lunch by the beach

    Spices, in The Repulse Bay arcade, has a separate vegan and vegetarian menu and outdoor tables. Classified, at The Pulse right on the sand, is a casual café with vegetarian options.

    Where109 Repulse Bay Road · The Pulse, 28 Beach Road

  3. 2:30 PM

    Spa day

    A couples massage and a long, slow afternoon. We'll pick the place later; holiday week books up, so reserve a couples room as soon as you choose.

  4. Evening

    Quiet night

    An early dinner near the hotel and a proper night's sleep before Disneyland.

No lifeguards at Repulse Bay in winter. They're off duty from December to February. Neighbouring Deep Water Bay is staffed through the winter, 8 AM–5 PM in past seasons.
迪士尼

Day 4 · Tuesday 29 December

Disneyland at Christmas

Lantau Island

  1. Opening

    Be at the gates before the park opens 香港迪士尼樂園

    "A Disney Christmas" runs from 20 November 2026 to 3 January 2027 and is included with admission. The week between Christmas and New Year is one of the park's busiest, so the first hour matters most. Go straight to World of Frozen, then work back toward Main Street.

    Getting thereMTR to Sunny Bay then change to the Disneyland Resort line · about 30 minutes from Hong Kong or Kowloon station

  2. 12:00 PM

    Lunch at Plaza Inn

    A Chinese table-service restaurant on Main Street with vegetarian dishes marked on the menu. Reserve online; walk-in waits will be long this week. For something quick, Starliner Diner in Tomorrowland has a veggie burger.

  3. Afternoon

    Christmas shows, then a break

    Mid-afternoon is the busiest stretch. Use it for the Christmas entertainment and a hot drink so you have energy left for the evening.

  4. Closing

    Momentous nighttime spectacular

    About 20 minutes of fireworks and projections over the castle. Find a spot on Main Street early. Check the park calendar for the showtime on the 29th.

Book the ticket and the date together, now. Every guest needs a park reservation for a specific day, and each ticket type has a daily quota that sells out in holiday weeks. Tickets are priced in four tiers by date; check the official calendar for the 29th. The park also holds its own New Year's Eve countdown at the castle, if you'd rather spend the 31st there.
大嶼山

Day 5 · Wednesday 30 December

Cable car, Big Buddha, Tai O

Lantau Island

  1. 10:00 AM

    Ngong Ping 360 cable car 昂坪360

    A 25-minute ride over the mountains and sea. Crystal cabins have glass floors; Crystal+ adds glass sides. Book online, up to 14 days ahead, so you skip the ticket queue in holiday week.

    Getting thereTung Chung Exit B, a 5-minute walk · runs about 10 AM–6 PM

  2. 11:00 AM

    Tian Tan Buddha 天壇大佛

    268 steps up to the giant bronze Buddha, with views across Lantau's peaks and the sea. The nearby Wisdom Path is closed for renovation until the end of 2026.

  3. 12:30 PM

    Vegetarian lunch at Po Lin Monastery 寶蓮禪寺

    A fixed vegetarian set (soup, a fried dish, vegetables, rice and tea) that changes with the season. Buy a meal ticket at the kitchen reception or the Buddha's entrance; the standard set is around HK$148 a person. Tofu pudding from the kiosk next door makes a good dessert.

    HoursKitchen 11:30 AM–4:30 PM

  4. 2:30 PM

    Tai O fishing village 大澳

    Stilt houses over the water, dried-seafood stalls and a much slower pace than the city.

    Getting thereBus 21 from Ngong Ping, about 15 minutes · bus back to Tung Chung

  5. Evening

    Quiet night in

    Rest up. Tomorrow ends after midnight.

New Year's Eve

Sham Shui Po, Mong Kok, then the harbour

  1. 10:00 AM

    Apliu Street and Golden Computer Arcade 鴨寮街

    An open-air electronics flea market (cables, chargers, cameras, odd gadgets), then floors of computer parts and accessories at the arcade on Fuk Wa Street. Compare prices at a few stalls before you buy.

    Getting thereSham Shui Po Exit C2 for Apliu Street

  2. 12:00 PM

    The fabric streets 基隆街

    Ki Lung, Nam Cheong and Yu Chau streets: bolts of fabric, trims, ribbons and cushion covers. Good for home projects and small decor finds.

  3. 1:30 PM

    Lunch at Three Virtues 三德素食館

    Vegetarian Cantonese food and dim sum; order the pan-fried turnip cake. Listings disagree on hours, so phone ahead, especially on a holiday eve.

    WhereJD Mall, 233–239 Nathan RoadJordan · tel. 3622 1888

  4. 2:45 PM

    Mong Kok, briefly 旺角

    Phones and accessories at Sin Tat Plaza, sneakers on Fa Yuen Street, and the Ladies' Market on Tung Choi Street for cheap clothes and souvenirs. Bargaining is normal at the stalls. Keep it to an hour or so.

    Getting thereMong Kok Exit E2 for the Ladies' Market

  5. 4:30 PM

    Back to the hotel

    Drop the bags, nap, shower and dress up. Roads around the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront start closing from early evening, so get back before then.

  6. 7:00 PM

    New Year's Eve dinner

    Book an early table close to the hotel. Kailash Parbat on Prat Avenue is a short walk from the waterfront; Harbour City stayed open until 12:30 AM on New Year's Eve last year if you want to browse after.

  7. Midnight

    The countdown 倒數

    Welcome 2027 on the harbour. Pick one of the three ways below and settle in by 10 PM.

New Year's Eve · 31 December 2026

Three ways to see in 2027

86 days, 21 hours until midnight in Hong Kong
Free

On the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront

The Avenue of Stars, the Cultural Centre piazza and the Star Ferry promenade face the Island skyline directly. Arrive at least two hours early. When viewing areas fill, police close them and switch to one-way foot traffic, and some exits at Tsim Sha Tsui and East Tsim Sha Tsui stations close.

Book ahead

On the water

Harbour cruises put you in the middle of it. Last year the Star Ferry's three-hour New Year's Eve harbour tour cost about HK$788, and Aqua Luna's junk boat, leaving around 10:30 PM, about HK$1,688. Book as soon as sailings go on sale.

Book ahead

Up high

Rooftop bars sell New Year's Eve tickets: OZONE at the Ritz-Carlton, on the Kowloon side, is the highest. Or skip the harbour and join the street party in Lan Kwai Fong, Central, which in 2025 was closed to traffic from 2 PM.

Will there be fireworks? Not yet announced. Last year's harbour fireworks were cancelled after the Tai Po fire and replaced by a countdown and light show in Central. The year before, twelve minutes of fireworks at midnight drew about 480,000 people. The National Day fireworks went ahead on 1 October 2026, and the Tourism Board usually confirms New Year's plans in mid-December.

Getting home. In recent years most MTR lines have run all night on New Year's Eve; the Airport Express and Disneyland line have not. From a Tsim Sha Tsui hotel, walking back is simplest.

Wan Chai and Causeway Bay · public holiday

Eating vegetarian

The vegetarian table

Hong Kong is easier for vegetarians than it looks, as long as you know where meat and fish hide. At ordinary Cantonese restaurants, stir-fried greens usually come with oyster sauce, and chicken powder and dried shrimp turn up as background seasoning. Dedicated vegetarian restaurants, marked 齋 or 素, remove the guesswork. In holiday week, book every dinner.

Two shopping days

What to buy where

Hong Kong has no sales tax, so the price on the tag is the price you pay. The morning of New Year's Eve covers Kowloon's markets and specialist streets; New Year's Day afternoon covers the Island's boutiques and malls, with decor stops on the 27th too.

Logistics

Getting around

The MTR gets you almost everywhere on this plan. Ferries, trams and buses fill the gaps, and taxis are cheap by US standards.

Pay for everything

Octopus card

Download the Octopus App for Tourists and add a card to Apple Pay with a US Visa or Mastercard (iPhone X or later). Or buy a physical Sold Tourist Octopus card (HK$39, then add value). It works on the MTR, buses, trams, the Star Ferry and at 7-Eleven. MTR gates also take contactless Visa, Mastercard and Apple Pay directly. The Peak Tram is the exception: buy a ticket at the counter.

Station chips throughout this page show the line colour, so you can match them to the signs. On New Year's Eve most lines have run all night in recent years.

Three colours

Taxis

Red, urban. Go almost anywhere, except Tung Chung Road and South Lantau.
Green, New Territories. Plus set routes to the airport and Disneyland.
Blue, Lantau. Covers the whole island, including Ngong Ping and Tai O.

Since April 2026 every taxi must accept electronic payment. A sticker in the window shows which methods that cab takes. Expect long taxi queues after midnight on New Year's Eve.

On the water and the rails

Star Ferry and trams

The Star Ferry runs between Central Pier 7 and Tsim Sha Tsui every 6–12 minutes, from early morning to about 11:30 PM. The double-decker trams run along the north shore of the Island; board at the back and pay as you leave. Both take Octopus.

Before you go

Practical notes

Entry

US passport holders can visit Hong Kong without a visa for up to 90 days.

Book now

Disneyland (ticket plus a dated park reservation), the spa, New Year's Eve cruise or rooftop tickets when they go on sale, New Year's Eve dinner, and the New Year's Day farewell dinner. Ngong Ping 360 opens bookings 14 days ahead.

Weather, late December

MonthHighLowSea
Dec69°F61°F68°F
Jan66°F58°F64°F

Hong Kong Observatory averages, 1991–2020. Dry and mild; pack a light jacket for evenings on the water.

Holidays and crowds

Christmas Day, Saturday 26 December and Friday 1 January are public holidays, and the whole week is peak season. Expect queues at the Peak, Disneyland and Ngong Ping, and book every dinner.

Christmas lights

WinterFest's Christmas Town in Statue Square, the East Tsim Sha Tsui building lights and Harbour City's decorations have all run into the first days of January in past years.

Money and tipping

Many restaurants add a 10% service charge, and no further tip is expected. Small cafés and stalls don't expect tips; rounding up a taxi fare is a nice gesture.

Power

UK-style three-pin plugs (Type G), 220V. Phone and laptop chargers are usually fine with an adapter; check anything that says 110V only.

The spa

Monday afternoon, 28 December, is set aside for it. Pick the place once the hotel is booked, so it's an easy trip from the beach or back to the room.

Confirm in December

Everything here was checked in October 2026. The New Year's Eve programme, the harbour light show and holiday opening hours are usually confirmed in December, so check the big ones the week before you fly.
